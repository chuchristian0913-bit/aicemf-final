Mastercard grew fastest and Visa turned the most revenue into cash; the two networks again out-earned the processors on every margin line.

What we compared

We standardised the annual filings of five listed payments businesses from SEC EDGAR XBRL, using each issuer's latest annual report filed on or before the information cutoff. Revenue, operating income, net income, operating cash flow and capital expenditure are taken as reported in the filings; every ratio below is computed from unrounded figures rather than from the rounded numbers printed here. Growth

Mastercard grew revenue 16.4% to $32.8bn, the fastest in the group, and its three-year compound rate of 13.8% is the only one above ten percent. Visa added 11.3% to $40.0bn, a three-year compound rate of 10.9%. PayPal and Fiserv grew 4.3% and 3.6% respectively, with compound rates of 6.4% and 6.1%. Global Payments reported -0.4% at $7.7bn; that figure is on a post-divestiture basis, so the two-year compound rate of 2.2% is the only growth measure comparable with the business as it now stands.

Profitability and cash generation

The networks remain a structurally different business from the processors. Visa earned an operating margin of 60.0% and a net margin of 50.1%, Mastercard 57.6% and 45.6%. Global Payments (22.8%) and Fiserv (27.5%) sit in the twenties and PayPal is lowest at 18.3%. Cash conversion is uniformly strong: every company converted more than its reported net income into free cash flow in the year, from 106.3% at PayPal to 145.6% at Global Payments, with Visa generating $21.6bn and Mastercard $17.2bn of free cash flow.

Valuation

Our equity DCF discounts three years of free cash flow at a cost of equity between 8.9% and 11.0%, and assumes terminal growth of 2.5%. On that basis Visa is worth $391.0bn of equity and Mastercard $315.2bn; per share, Mastercard $347.92, Fiserv $127.42, Global Payments $119.91 and PayPal $83.79. Visa's per-share value is withheld: the company does not tag a diluted share count in its XBRL filings, and we do not estimate an input we cannot source.
